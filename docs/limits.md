# Limits

[← README](../README.md) · [Using the guard](using.md) · [The running print](running-print.md) · [Learning](learning.md) · [Spools without a reading](manual-amounts.md) · **Limits**

What the guard cannot read or does not yet know, and what it does in each case.

- A running print is counted only when it was sliced on this computer while the guard (1.3.0 or later) was watching, and its G-code adds up to Bambu Studio's totals (see [The running print](running-print.md)). Otherwise the card says it could not count it, and the amounts are what the spools hold now.
- A job is matched to its slice by project name and layer count, and by plate when the printer names the file it prints (`/data/Metadata/plate_2.gcode`, as the H2D does). A printer that does not (an A1 mini printing from its SD card reports nothing there) gets the newer of two plates of one project with the same number of layers. Re-slicing a plate replaces its slice: from then on, a print still running from the earlier slice is counted with the new slice's amounts, or not at all when the layer count changed.
- What the running print needs beyond its tray is taken off the loaded spools AMS auto refill would go on with, as the guard reads that rule (the same material and colour, the same product where both name one, the same nozzle). Whether auto refill is on, and which spool the AMS really takes, cannot be read.
- Each plate is checked on its own when it is sliced, and at "Send print job". "Print all plates" needs the sum: export the sliced file and run `check` on it. For a file with several plates, `check FILE` answers for each plate and then for all plates together, with each filament's grams added up over the plates and judged against the same reading of the printer; its exit code is the worst of those answers. A plate whose G-code is not complete in the file "could not be checked", and then neither can all plates together.
- Which plate "Send print job" is sending, and which tray you pick there, cannot be read: the card beside it is the worst of the project's sliced plates, against every matching spool. Bambu Studio's own warning there ("The filament in … may be insufficient for this print") covers the chosen tray.
- Filaments that can draw on a spool in common are judged on what they need together: two slots of one colour, and also filaments that share only one of their spools (a spool that stands in for another product, the external spool for both nozzles, two shades within the colour tolerance of one spool). Which of them takes the shared spool cannot be read, so each is judged against its own spools with the others' needs added.
- A filament whose profile has density 0 (a profile made by hand, until a density is entered) gets 0.00 g from Bambu Studio, however much of it the plate prints. The guard does not guess its weight: the filament's result is "unknown" and says why, a filament that can print from the same spool is "unknown" as well and names it, and a plate with no other filament "could not be checked". Such a plate gets no per-layer profile, so a print of it is not counted while it runs, and an amount entered for the spool that filament prints from becomes out of date when the print starts.
- With two printers of one model, one is asked: the one selected in Bambu Studio, or, when the printer selected there is of another model, the first of that model in Bambu Studio's configuration. The other is not asked. The card, the one-line card, the log and the text output then name the printer that answered by the last four characters of its serial number, as in "Bambu Lab P1S (…0002)": check that it is the one the job goes to.
- Printers are the ones Bambu Studio holds a LAN access code for, each of the model Bambu Studio's own list of printers gives for its serial number. A plate sliced for a model none of them is, is logged and shows no card; where only one printer is known, one line says so ("Not checked: sliced for a …, your printer is a …"), since the wrong printer preset is the likely cause. A printer whose serial number that list does not name (a model newer than the installed Bambu Studio) cannot be matched to any plate: a card says so when a plate matches no other printer, and the self-test fails on it.
- The guard counts the amount the printer reports for a spool: grams where the firmware gives them, else its percentage of the spool's full weight. With "Update remaining capacity" switched off in the AMS settings, printers are reported to send no amount, and the guard then says "unknown". Whether any firmware keeps sending an old figure with that option off has not been verified on a printer.
- A printer sends its status in pieces. The guard waits up to three seconds for the piece with the spools (the list of AMS units, which may be empty); a status that still has none is not judged: the card says the printer did not report its spools, and no amount entered by hand ends on it.
- Which nozzle an AMS feeds is read as Bambu Studio reads it (the extruder the AMS says it is bound to) and, where the printer does not say it there, as the H2D tells it (firmware 01.04.20.00). On a printer with two nozzles an external spool holder counts for its own nozzle only, told by its id (Bambu Studio's "Ext-L" and "Ext-R"; the guard's card calls them Ext and Ext2). Where the nozzle cannot be read, a spool counts for either nozzle. Only the H2D's own report has been seen.
- How a job names an external spool in its tray mapping has not been seen on a print; a tray the guard cannot name makes the entered amounts out of date, never wrong.
- Learning learns nothing from a print not sliced on this computer. From a print it followed only from its middle (the guard started during it) it learns a run-dry but no rate and no loss before the first layer, which need the print's start. A spool moved to another tray starts again there: what is learned belongs to the tray, not the spool.
- Learning takes the readings the printer reports to follow the spool between the guard's looks, 3 seconds apart. Not yet seen on a print, and recorded so that the history will show them: how often a tray's reading changes during a print (a reading that lags shows as a low rate; a print's rate is clipped at 0.9); what a tray looks like in the status when its spool runs out (counted as a run-dry only when the tray is reported without a spool in two looks in a row; a tray still reported, reading 0 g, makes the print's record unsound instead, with the reading before it); whether the AMS, refilling from a second spool, changes the job's tray mapping (the record then says at which layer, and is not used); and whether the reading settles after the print ends (both the first reading at the end and the one a minute later are kept).
- The measured reserve (experimental, off unless turned on) rests on the next print and the next spool being like the ones it was measured on. A change in how far a tray's reading falls per gram is caught once its forecast errors show it. A change in what spools read at empty is not: it never shows in forecast errors, and only new run-dries, each a print that ran out, move it. Its two bounds of one in ten, for forecasts that fall short and for spools that read more at empty, each hold over many prints or many spools together, not for one tray or one size of print; and the bound for one more spool's reading at empty takes spools to differ as a Normal spread does, measured over the printer's trays together, so a tray unlike the others is not covered by it. See [Learning](learning.md#what-the-measured-reserve-cannot-see). A false run-dry (a spool taken out at 50 g or less while the print draws on it) stays in the tray's evidence until enough spools are seen feeding below it.
- The packaged guard is a folder, not one program. A single self-installing `.exe` was built first; Windows Defender removed it at its first start as `Trojan:Win32/Bearfoos.A!ml`, as it does with many unsigned packed programs that add themselves to Startup. The folder runs on python.org's signed `pythonw.exe` and is left alone.
