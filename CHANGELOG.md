## [1.5.0] - 2026-11-24

### Added

- A weigh-in more than 50 g from the printer's reading, either way, is not saved at the first Save. The window says both numbers and how far apart they are; Save again takes the weight as right. A slipped key or another spool on the scale was kept as what the tray reads at empty, and counted every tray of the printer lower.
- `learned --forget-weigh TRAY` takes back that tray's latest weigh-in and leaves the rest of what was learned (`--printer` where two printers have the tray).

### Changed

- The measured reserve is off unless you turn it on: "Measure the reserve from past prints (experimental)" in the icon's settings (`measured_reserve`). 1.4.0 had it on. It loosens a check on evidence that cannot see a change in what spools read at empty: in an independent review's simulation, once spools read 25 g more at empty, 5 of the 31 answers it had loosened to "enough" ran dry, where 1.3.0's rules had no failure. Nothing of the learning has yet followed a real print. Off, no answer is looser than the fixed reserve and 1.3.0's rules give: learning only makes a check stricter. Where the printer's past forecasts fell further short than the fixed reserve covers, the larger figure is the reserve, on or off, and the card says so: against 66,000 random checks no answer is looser than 1.4.0's either, with the setting as it comes or turned on. `docs/learning.md` has the cases and the numbers, under "What the measured reserve cannot see".
- The reserve's share of a spool's full weight (3 %) is taken from the largest spool the filament could be printed from, not from the fullest one. With a 250 g and a 1 kg spool of one filament loaded, that share is 30 g whichever is the fullest, so a check can be "tight" where it was "enough".
- No weigh-in is offered while a job is on the printer: the readings are moving then.
- The window for a spool's amount ("Amount in …", "Weigh C3…") takes what the scale shows and what the empty spool weighs, and takes one from the other: the filament shows as you type. It starts with the empty spool's weight entered last (`empty_spool_g`, 250 g at first); 0 there is the filament weighed alone, as before.
- A command line that cannot be read (a mistyped option, an unknown command) ends with exit code 3, "not checked", for every command. Python's own code for it is 2, which the README gives as "not enough" for `check`. `check --times` takes 1 or more; 0 and negative numbers, which read "enough" with exit code 0, are such a command line.
- For the reserve's share of a spool's full weight, a spool whose full weight the printer does not report, and a spool whose amount was entered by hand, count as a 1 kg spool: 30 g at 3 %. Such a spool had only 10 % of the need for a reserve, or 3 % of the amount first entered, so a check of it can be "tight" where it was "enough".
- "Forget what was learned" asks first, on a card with "Forget" and "Keep", and has moved from beside Quit into the icon's settings: one slip of the mouse emptied what a spool run dry and weeks of prints had taught. Forgetting everything, there or with `learned --forget`, keeps the history as it was as `learned.json.forgotten` beside `learned.json`: one copy, replaced the next time, and the card and the command say where it is.

### Fixed

- With learning on, a check could answer "enough" where 1.3.0's rules said "tight", before any reserve was measured. Which spool is the fullest changes with what was learned of the trays: a 1 kg spool counted lower for another tray's run-dry left a 250 g spool the fullest, and the reserve fell from 30 g to 7.5 g. The reserve no longer depends on which spool is the fullest.
- One spool weighed near empty three times, or weighed and then run dry, counted as three readings at empty. The measured reserve could then be earned on how a scale repeats, with no second spool seen. A spool now counts once, by its largest reading at empty; no tray is counted higher for it.
- The card could say "the reserve is measured" when the row had used the fixed one (scored prints in one tray, run-dries only in another). With the measured reserve on, it now names the reserve its rows used and, for the fixed one, what the measured one still lacks, in a sentence that can be read. With it off the card says nothing of the reserve.
- A `learned.json` with one odd record (a key missing, None or text where a number belongs, a list where a record belongs) made every check of that printer end as "could not be checked", and `learned` crash on the same file. Such a record is now left out and the rest of the history used; the log says once what was left out. Whatever else goes wrong in what was learned, the check goes on as with learning off, and the card and the text output say that what was learned could not be used.
- A warning that offered a weigh-in could fail to show. The card noted the offer in the state file before it was put on the screen; when another program held that file, the note failed and the card stayed unseen. The card is now shown first, and a note that cannot be written is logged: the offer may then come again.
- Two filaments that could print from the same spool were added up only when all their spools were the same. With one spool in common and another apart (a spool standing in for a product that is not loaded, the external spool for both nozzles, two shades within the colour tolerance of one spool), each was judged alone, and both could read "enough" on a spool that does not hold both. Each filament is now judged against its own spools with the need of every filament it shares one with added.
- "Needs two spools: AMS auto refill must be on" was given across any matching spools that held the need together, also an AMS tray and the external spool, or two products 31 apart in colour, which the AMS never goes on between. It is now given only across spools of the same product and colour that are both in an AMS; a total reached only with another spool is "not enough", and the note names that spool and its amount.
- A spool of similar colour but another product counts when no spool of the project's own product is loaded, and did so without a word: PLA Basic red read "enough" on a PLA Silk+ spool. It still counts, since it can be picked at "Send print job", and the filament's note now names it whatever the result: "B3 is PLA Silk+, another product than the project's Bambu PLA Basic". `docs/using.md` said such a spool is never counted, and now says when it is.
- `check FILE` on a file with several plates judged each plate alone: three plates of 400 g against one 1 kg spool read "enough" three times, with exit code 0, although `docs/limits.md` sends there for "Print all plates". It now adds one more answer, "all plates together": each filament's grams added up over the plates and judged against the same reading of the printer, printed last, in `--json` too, and counted in the exit code.
- A filament in use whose weight Bambu Studio writes as 0.00 g (its profile has density 0) was dropped without a word: a two-colour plate read "enough" on the other colour alone, a plate with no other filament showed no card and `check` answered "enough" with exit code 0, and an amount entered for its spool was never counted down. Such a filament is now "unknown", with the note "Bambu Studio gives no weight for this filament (its profile's density is 0)"; a plate with no filament the guard can weigh "could not be checked" (exit code 3); and a job that draws such a filament from a spool with an entered amount makes that amount out of date.
- `check` ended in a Python traceback and exit code 1, which the README gives as "tight", for a `.gcode.3mf` that is damaged, cut short or encrypted, for a newest slice it could not read, and for text output through a pipe when the project's name has a character outside the console's code page. Each now ends in one line and exit code 3, as does any other error inside the check; a character the console cannot show prints as "?". With `--json`, the exits without an answer (no slice, a file that cannot be read) print `{"error": "…"}`, where they printed plain text.
- The guard looked for slices under the temp folder Python goes by (`%TEMP%` before `%TMP%`); Bambu Studio writes under the one Windows gives it (`%TMP%` before `%TEMP%`). With only one of the two moved, say `TMP` to a RAM disk, no plate was ever checked and nothing said so; a temp folder with `[` or `]` in its path had the same effect. The guard now looks under the folder Windows names, under `%TMP%` and under `%TEMP%`, each as it is spelt.
- A line of the G-code's header with nothing after its colon was read with the next line as its value: a slice with an empty `; filament:` line was reported as one the guard cannot read, though its totals can be placed, and an empty totals line gave a reason that named another line. Such a line is now empty.
- A filament total of `nan` or `inf` in a G-code was taken as a number, and the filament's answer read "short by nan g". A slice with such a total is now one the guard cannot read, and it says which total.
- `check FILE` left a plate whose G-code is not complete in the file out without a word, and answered for the others as if they were the whole file. Such a plate is now an answer of its own, "could not be checked", all plates together have none, and the exit code is 3.
- A filament that fell short could show as one line, and not at all beside "Send print job". The full card opened by the plate's worst result alone: with "Warn when a colour is not loaded" unticked, a filament whose colour was not loaded hid the shortfall of the one after it, and a `popup_on` written by hand without `SHORT` (`["UNKNOWN"]`, or names in small letters) turned the "not enough" card off. Any one filament whose result opens the card now opens it; "not enough" and "not loaded" do whatever `popup_on` holds, which decides only for colour, two spools, tight and unknown. Its names are read in capitals or small letters, and the log says once when one of them is no result to warn of (`OK` is none: "enough" shows as one line).
- "Ignore for this project" was kept for ever, per project title, as the filaments' results alone: a project ignored at 5 g short stayed one line at 370 g short, on every plate, and every project that was never saved shares one title, so one ignore muted them all. An ignore is now kept per plate, with its date and what each filament fell short by. It ends when a result changes, when a filament falls more than 5 g further short, and after 7 days. A project never saved is told by the folder Bambu Studio keeps its slices in. An ignore kept by an earlier version has lapsed.
- "Send print job" checked the 8 plates sliced last, where the README and the docs said every sliced plate: of 12 plates sliced in one go, a first plate that needed 900 g of a 500 g spool showed nothing beside the dialog. Every sliced plate of the project is now checked there. Of more than 30, the 30 sliced last are, and what shows beside the dialog says how many older ones were not: on the warning, or on a card of its own when there is nothing else to warn of.
- At "Send print job" one plate was chosen as the worst before it was known whether it would show. A plate whose finding was ignored, or whose result was not one to warn of, could be chosen over a plate that fell short, and nothing showed. A plate that could not be checked ranked below "enough": one reading of the printer that timed out dropped the plate being sent when the next plate was fine. The plates are now ranked by what would open a card, the worst first; a plate that could not be checked comes above enough and unknown, and its card shows when no plate warns. A check that fails is tried once more, and a printer that does not answer is asked twice, not once for every plate.
- A plate's warning card was replaced by another plate's "Enough" line once two minutes had passed, and at once by a fine slice in a second Bambu Studio or of another unsaved project. The line went after 8 seconds and the icon read "Enough filament for this print". A warning's card now gives way only to a new answer for its own plate in its own Bambu Studio, to a worse answer, to a warning beside "Send print job", and to what you ask for; a plate that could not be checked stands the same way. While it stands the icon keeps its colour, and the icon's tooltip names the plate.
- One write to `guard.log` that failed (the disk full, the file held by another program) lost the answer it was about, and could end the watching for good: the icon stayed, no slice was checked, and the guard could no longer be stopped. Writing the log now never fails a check: the lines lost are counted and said with the next line written, an answer is on its way to the card before it is logged, the watcher and the card's loop go on whatever one round of theirs does, and a watcher that has ended is started again. A `poll_seconds` that is not a positive number is read as 2.
- The window for a spool's amount closed before the amount was stored: with the state file held by another program the weight typed was gone, and only the log said so. It now stays open and says "Not saved" until the amount is stored. The empty spool's weight is kept after the amount, and a `config.json` that cannot be written no longer costs the amount: the weight is then used until the guard stops. An empty spool of 0, or left blank, is not kept as the next window's start, where it put a later spool's 250 g on as filament; while the field holds it, the result reads "612 g of filament: nothing taken off for the spool". On the card of `check --popup` with no guard running, the window says that the amount is counted down only while the guard runs.
- A warning could end as a line in the log, with no card, when another program held the state file at the moment the guard looked up whether the finding was ignored. What cannot be looked up counts as not ignored, and the card shows.
- "Show last result", the icon's click, put the last answer on a card as it was: hours old, after a print that had drawn on the spools, it still read "Enough", "as the printer reports them now". The click now checks that plate again and shows the fresh answer ("Check last plate again" in the menu), one check however often it is clicked. A card made from an answer more than a minute old says when the printer reported its spools, in place of "now".
- A warning found when "Send print job" opened was thrown away when the dialog had closed before the check ended, which a quick click on Send also looks like. It now shows as a card in the corner, as after a slice.
- "Check newest slice now" while checks were paused turned the icon green, as if slices were being checked. Paused, the icon stays grey and its tooltip says "Checks are paused"; resumed, it takes the last result's colour again.
- The window for a spool's amount opened with the amount on the card, which is as old as the card: a spool entered at 400 g and counted down to 280 g opened at 400 g, and Save as it stood, or Return to close the window, wrote 400 g back over the count. During a print that figure was then taken off a second time. The window opens with what the guard counts for the spool at that moment, and Save with both fields as they opened enters nothing.
- A weigh-in was set against the reading on the card it came from, however old. A card from before a print: C3 read 105 g there, the spool weighed at 20 g afterwards was kept as one that reads 105 g with 20 g on it, and every tray of the printer was counted 85 g lower. When a weigh-in is saved the printer is now asked again: it is kept, against the reading the tray gives then, only when no job is on the printer and the tray holds the same spool within 2 g of the reading the window showed. Otherwise nothing is kept and a card says why.
- With Bambu Studio on a screen left of the primary one, or above it, the window for a spool's amount opened at the edge of the primary screen, a screen away from the card that was clicked. It opens above the card, within the screen the card is on.
- While a warning stood beside "Send print job", the guard kept about 6 kB more memory at every look at that dialog, 2.5 times a second: some 57 MB an hour. It no longer does.
- A filament whose colour in the G-code is not six hexadecimal digits (a project made by hand) could keep its plate's warning from showing at all. Its square on the card is now a neutral one.
- The one-line card could be wider than a small Bambu Studio window when it told of the print running on the printer, with its left part off the screen. Its text now wraps.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-11-20

### Added

- Learning scores itself. When a print it followed ends, the guard keeps what it had forecast the tray's reading would fall by, by what it knew before that print, and what the reading fell. From 9 scored prints, and readings at empty that repeat within a tray, the reserve is measured from those errors (a one-sided 90 % conformal bound, per gram of print plus 20 g, never below 5 g or 2 % of the need) instead of the fixed one, and can be smaller: a tray that proves accurate is no longer called "tight" on 3 % of its spool. A new setting, "Measure the reserve from past prints" (`measured_reserve`, on), turns it off. Until it is earned, or with it off, no answer is looser than 1.3.0's.
- "Weigh C3…" on the card, when weighing a spool the printer measures would settle the answer: at most once a week per tray and once a day in all. The spool is then counted with no more than it held; weighed near empty, it tells what the tray reads at empty.
- A tray whose learned forecasts do clearly worse than the slicer's grams (a sign test over its last 10) has them set aside; a change in a tray's forecast errors (Bayesian online change-point detection) starts what is learned of it again.
- `learned` shows the scores, the rate's 90 % interval, the evidence for what each tray reads at empty, which reserve is in use, any change noticed, and the rate by filament product.

### Changed

- The rate and the loss before the first layer are used from the first print, each tray's drawn toward the printer's own figure as far as its trays cannot be told apart, recent prints weighing more than old ones, and each print's ratio clipped first so that one odd print cannot set it. A rate above 1 now adds to the need.
- What a tray reads with its spool empty is an upper bound from run-dries, spools weighed near empty, and spools seen still feeding at a reading (Kaplan-Meier), in place of the largest of the last 3 run-dries and the rule that dropped a run-dry at once when a later spool fed lower.
- A filament's need takes the largest learned rate and loss of the trays it could be printed from.
- `learned.json` keeps 100 records per tray (the newest 20 with their readings layer by layer), 20 run-dries and 20 weigh-ins. A 1.3.0 file reads as it is; its records are not scored, and scoring starts with the next print.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-11-20

### Added

- Counts the print that is running. From each spool the guard first takes off what the print on the printer still needs from it, from the layer the printer is on to the end, so the answer is for the moment the checked print could start. What it needs beyond its tray comes off the other loaded spools of that filament, as AMS auto refill would; when none holds it, the card warns that the running print needs more than is loaded. In batch printing the next plate is sliced while one prints; until now it was checked against spools the running print was still emptying.
- Keeps, for each plate it sees sliced, the grams of each filament every layer takes, counted the way Bambu Studio counts its totals: the moves along X or Y that extrude, and the purge at each filament change (moves in `FLUSH` blocks, or `;VG1` comments in `VFLUSH` blocks), of which as much as the nozzle holds counts for the filament pushed out. Measured on Bambu Studio 02.09.01.70 slices for the A1, P1, X1, H2, P2S, A2L, X2D and H3C families: every filament within 0.005 g of Bambu Studio's total. A slice that does not add up gets no profile, and the log says by how much. The last 30 are kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\BambuFilamentGuard\profiles\`, the purge apart from the rest.
- The card, the text report and `--json` say whether the running print was counted, and a filament's note names the spool's reading and what the running print still needs of it.
- A line that says a check is under way ("Checking the spools on the Bambu Lab H2D…") as soon as one starts that you are waiting for: a new slice, or a check from the icon. The answer takes its place; a newest slice that cannot be read ends in a card that says so.
- Learns from previous prints, per printer and tray, how far that tray's reading can be trusted: what it read when a spool ran dry in it, how far its reading falls per gram the tray gives, and what it loses before the first layer. A check counts the tray with less, or the need with more, where that history shows it; it can only make a check stricter, and an amount entered by hand is left alone. A run-dry counts only for a print sliced here, from a tray the print was drawing on, reported empty twice, having read 50 g or less; a later print that shows the tray still feeding at a lower reading retires it. While learning is on (setting `learn`, on by default) the guard follows a printer it has checked, or that prints, over the same read-only link as for entered amounts. `learned` shows what was learned and on what evidence; `learned --forget` and the icon's "Forget what was learned" start again.
- A line when the guard starts, the first time Bambu Studio runs: "Bambu Filament Guard 1.3.0 is watching for sliced plates". After `Install.cmd` its own card says it instead.

### Changed

- A job is matched to its slice by plate as well, when the printer names the file it prints, so two plates of one project with the same number of layers are told apart. The amounts entered by hand are counted down by the same match.
- A new slice is checked before it is remembered, so a plate sliced again while it prints is checked against the slice that print was made from.

### Fixed

- A click on the icon while its check was running started another check, five in one second in one log; now it shows the "Checking…" line and starts nothing.
- A plate whose check failed ended that round of the watcher, and plates sliced with it went unchecked.
- The printer's job showed "None %" when the printer gave no percentage.
- An amount entered by hand while a print ran never had the rest of that print taken off it; now it has, at the guard's next look at the printer.
- The watcher and a command run beside it could undo each other's changes to the state file, or have it set aside as damaged, losing entered amounts. The state folder's files are now read and written by one at a time, across processes, and a write that cannot be made now is tried again rather than lost.
- At "Send print job", one sliced plate of the project that could not be read stopped the check of all of them; it is now left out, and logged.
- A one-line card that came while Bambu Studio was minimised counted its seconds from then, and could go before it was ever seen; it now counts from when it shows.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-11-19

The first public release.

### Added

- Reads the grams each filament needs from the G-code Bambu Studio writes for every sliced plate, purge and prime tower included.
- Asks the printer, read-only over the LAN, what is left on every spool, and shows a "Filament check" card inside Bambu Studio's window: one line when there is enough, the full table when a filament falls short.
- Results: enough, tight, needs two spools, not enough, not loaded, colour not loaded, unknown. "Enough" includes a reserve, because the AMS's figure is an estimate.
- Checks every sliced plate again when "Send print job" opens, and shows the worst answer beside that dialog.
- Amounts for spools the printer cannot measure, entered on the card and counted down as prints run.
- A notification-area icon with the last result and the settings; `check`, `status`, `trays`, `selftest`, `install` and `uninstall` commands, with exit codes a script can gate on.
- A packaged folder and zip with a Python of its own (`python build.py`), for a computer that has none.
